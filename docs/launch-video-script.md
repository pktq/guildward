# "The Door": Guildward launch film (v2, explainer)

**Runtime:** about 3:05 (16:9 master, 4:5 for LinkedIn).
**Audience:** parents and other adults, mostly on LinkedIn, X and Facebook, many of them watching muted.
**Job of the film:** make an adult understand three things. What sextortion is. Why their child can't easily see it coming. Where it gets in, and how Guildward closes each of those gaps. It's an explainer with a heart, not a product launch.

**The one rule for everything Guildward says, here and everywhere else:** never shame the child, and always tell parents not to. The blackmailer's only power is the child's fear that their parents will find out. A child who knows they won't be ridiculed or punished can't be controlled. As long as the child is safe, nothing is ruined. Every scene, post, reply and product string follows this.

**Core image:** a closed bedroom door at night. Parents think it keeps danger out. In the middle of the film we see light leaking through the gaps around it; each gap is a way in. Guildward closes four of them. The last one, the gap under the door, only a parent can close.

**Voices**
- **NARRATOR:** calm, plain, like a good teacher. Carries most of the film.
- **MOM:** used only at the start and the end, so the film opens and closes on a person rather than a product.

**Look:** soft 2D, night palette. The chat app is a **generic, made-up UI**, not Discord's.

## 2. What sextortion is (0:12–0:40)

**VISUAL:** The word **SEXTORTION** appears, then splits into *sex* + *extortion*. A simple diagram builds: a phone sends a photo card, a lock snaps onto it, and arrows run from the lock to three icons: *friends*, *family*, *school*.

> **NARRATOR:** Sextortion is blackmail.
> Someone gets a child to share an intimate photo or video, usually by pretending to be someone their own age.
> Then they threaten to send it to everyone the child knows.

**VISUAL:** Two cards slide in side by side.
- **For money.** Organised groups running scripts. They demand cash or gift cards, within hours. Most targets are boys aged 14 to 17.
- **For control.** Groups that demand more images, more humiliation, even self-harm. Some targets are younger than 12.

> **NARRATOR:** Some want money. Some want control.
> Both work the same way, and both start with a friendly message.

## 3. How it works (0:40–1:15)

**VISUAL:** Four steps across the top, lighting up one by one: **Meet → Trust → Move → Trap.** Below them, a chat window shows LEO (14) talking to **mia 🌸**.

**Meet.**
> *saw u in the server, ur actually so funny lol*

> **NARRATOR:** It starts where kids already are: a gaming server, a fan server, a homework server. The account is new. The photo is stolen.

**Trust.**
> *how old r u?* → *14* → *omg same-ish 😊 15*

> **NARRATOR:** Compliments. Shared games. And, early on, a question about age.

**Move.**
> *this server is so public lol. add me on snap? 👻*

> **NARRATOR:** Then a move somewhere private, where no moderator can see.

**Trap.** Blurred, flirty bubbles, then a **blank white card** leaves Leo's phone. The avatar glitches away. Behind it is a silhouette at a desk with six screens, each one another child's chat with the **same messages, copy-pasted**.
> *i have ur pic. i have ur followers. ur school. ur mom.*
> *$500 or everyone sees it. u have 2 hours.* ⏱

> **NARRATOR:** There was never a Mia. There was a stranger with a script, and dozens of other kids in other windows.
> From first message to threat can take one evening.

## 4. Why kids are so exposed (1:15–1:40)

**VISUAL:** Black screen. Numbers count up, one at a time.
> **50,000+** reports in 2025, in the US alone
> **137** a day
> Most targets: **boys, 14 to 17**
> Since 2021: **more than three dozen** teenage boys have taken their own lives after being targeted

> **NARRATOR:** It isn't because they're careless. They're fourteen. They want to be liked, and the person on the other side does this for a living.
> And when it goes wrong, they're more afraid of *you* finding out than of the person threatening them.
> That fear is the only power the blackmailer has. Take it away, and they lose their grip.

**VISUAL:** Quick vignettes of the quiet signs, each with a soft hand-drawn circle: a phone face-down at dinner · "can I borrow some money?" · flinching at a buzz · "just tired."

> **NARRATOR:** The signs are quiet. They look like a normal week.

## 5. Where it gets in (1:40–2:00)

**VISUAL:** Back to the bedroom door, now big and centred. One by one, cracks around the frame light up cold blue, each with a label.

> **NARRATOR:** A closed door feels safe. But there are gaps.

1. **Strangers can reach them.** Anyone in a shared server can send a friend request or a message, depending on their settings.
2. **Anyone can pretend.** A new account and a stolen photo look exactly like a real 15-year-old.
3. **The playbook looks like normal chat.** Compliments, age questions, "add me on snap": nothing in it looks like danger to a kid.
4. **Some servers exist to find kids.** And an invite link looks the same whoever sent it.
5. **No one is watching.** Most servers are run by volunteers who can't read every message.

---

## 6. What Guildward does (2:00–2:40)

**VISUAL:** Each crack goes dark as it's closed, with a small, real screen from Guildward beside it. These are plain UI moments, not product glamour shots.

> **NARRATOR:** Guildward is a safety tool for Discord. It closes these gaps one at a time.

1. **Strangers can reach them →** *Lockdown guide.* You and your child go through their settings together: DMs from servers off, friend requests limited, message requests on, sensitive images blurred.
2. **Anyone can pretend →** *Safety check.* Right-click any person to see how old the account is and any warning signs, explained in words a kid understands.
3. **The playbook →** *Is this sketchy?* Right-click any message. Guildward recognises the patterns: age questions, secrecy, moving to another app, photo requests, pressure.
4. **Servers →** *Check an invite* before joining. See the server's age, its settings and any red flags.
5. **No one watching →** *Guard,* for server owners. It alerts moderators when an adult asks lots of kids their age, pushes them to other apps, or asks for photos.

> **NARRATOR:** It doesn't read private messages, and it isn't spyware. It's a tool your child holds, with a warning at the exact moment it matters, and the steps to take next.

## 7. The last gap (2:40–3:05)

**VISUAL:** Every crack is dark except the strip of light **under the door**.

> **NARRATOR:** There's one gap no app can close.

**ON SCREEN + MOM (V.O.):**
> **"Whatever happens online, you can tell me. You won't be in trouble."**

> **NARRATOR:** If your child does tell you, how you react is everything.
> No shame. No punishment. As long as they're safe, nothing is ruined.

**CARD (for parents):**
> **If your child tells you**
> 1. Stay calm. Thank them for telling you.
> 2. Don't ask why they sent it.
> 3. Don't take their phone away as punishment.
> 4. Say: "We'll fix this together."
> 5. Then report it, together.
>
> **They're safe. That's what matters.**

**VISUAL:** The door opens and warm hallway light floods the room. Leo stands in the doorway.
> **LEO:** Mom? Can I show you something?

**CARD (for any kid watching):**
> **If this is happening to you:** Don't pay. Don't reply. Save the messages. Block. Tell an adult you trust. **It is not your fault.**
> Remove images: **takeitdown.ncmec.org** · Sweden: **Bris 116 111** · US: **report.cybertip.org**

**END CARD:** Guildward logo. **Be the one they tell.** Starting on Discord. *[URL]*

## Production notes (please don't skip)

- **Safe messaging on suicide.** Only the statistic in scene 4. Never show or imply a method, and never make Leo's fate explicit. Keep the help card at the end and in every post. Follow the [WHO media guidance on suicide](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240076846).
- **No sexual content of any kind.** The "photo" is a blank card. Blurred bubbles only.
- **The offender is a silhouette.** No nationality, ethnicity or accent.
- **Discord branding.** Saying "Discord" in the voiceover is fine, but don't recreate its UI, logo or Blurple, and don't suggest Discord endorses Guildward. Check [Discord's brand guidelines](https://discord.com/branding).
- **NCMEC.** We cite their numbers. We don't use their logo or suggest a partnership.
- **Captions** burned in on every social cut, and an SRT for YouTube and LinkedIn. **Swedish version:** dub both voices; localise currency and the help line.
- **No shame, anywhere.** Not in the film, the captions, the posts or the replies to comments. Leo is never shown as foolish, and no adult in the film is angry with him. If a commenter blames a child ("they shouldn't have sent it"), reply kindly and repeat that it's never the child's fault.
- **Comments will include real stories.** Pin the help card as a comment, and have a short reply ready ("Thank you for sharing. If it's happening now: don't pay, save everything, report at report.cybertip.org").
